The governance layer

Approvals are not one of the eight specialists. They're a structural governance layer enforced by the control plane itself — which is a stronger design than making approvals an agent's job. Every post the team produces carries a risk tier. Here's how it actually runs:

Calibration period · first two weeks

Every single post across every single platform is manually reviewed and approved — by us, not by you. This is the DNA-setting phase: Jimmy sits with every draft, flags anything that sounds off-brand or off-tone, and locks in the voice before anything scales. You can watch everything happen in real time through your dashboard if you want to, but you are not the gatekeeper. Your job is business. Our job is to dial in the system so that when it hits steady state, you can trust it.

Steady state · month 3 onwards

Low-risk content — educational explainers, seasonal reminders, community posts, product-category content — auto-publishes on schedule. Logged. 24-hour reversibility window.

Medium-risk content — pricing mentions, before/afters, branded promotions, seasonal offers — auto-publishes too, and is flagged to Jimmy for same-day review. We keep watch on what goes out; you don't have to.

High-risk content — anything touching insurance claims, named customers, legal or liability topics — is the only tier that requires your explicit approval before publishing. You get a notification, a one-tap approve/reject/edit on your phone, no keyboard required. These should be rare.

The default experience is hands-off. You are not expected to spend time approving routine posts. You're expected to run your roofing business. But you keep the keys — at any time, from Telegram, you can tell the Custom Content Agent to stop a scheduled post, pull down one that's already live, pause any platform, or hit the master kill switch. Say "pull the post about the Beaufort job" and it's gone in under ten seconds, logged, reversible. It's not a personality; it's a rule enforced by the platform.
