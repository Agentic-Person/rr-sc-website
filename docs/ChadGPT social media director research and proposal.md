## Why This Matters for a Roofing Company

This matters because homeowners do not make contractor decisions in one place anymore. They move between your website, your social presence, your reviews, and local recommendations before they ever call.

BrightLocal’s 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, the average consumer uses six different review sites when choosing businesses, and 54% are likely to visit a business’s website after reading positive reviews. The same survey also found that Google, Facebook, and AI tools are now among the most commonly used sources for local recommendations, and that 34% of consumers wrote a Facebook review in the past year. That is a strong argument for keeping your website, Facebook presence, and reputation signals active and aligned. ([BrightLocal][2])

There is also a broader market tailwind here. Harvard’s Joint Center for Housing Studies projected that U.S. spending on home renovation and repair would increase in 2025 and revised the 2025 remodeling market size up to **$509 billion**. That does not mean every dollar goes to roofing, but it does mean homeowners are still spending on their homes, and contractors who stay visible and credible online are better positioned to win attention. ([Joint Center for Housing Studies][3])

## Why This Matters in Charleston, South Carolina

Charleston gives this strategy even more substance because the market has a natural storm-readiness and roof-resilience conversation built into it.

South Carolina’s official **SC Safe Home Mitigation Grant Program** is specifically aimed at residents in designated coastal counties and supports stronger roofs, high-wind-rated roof coverings, secondary water barriers, reinforced roof-to-wall connections, and related mitigation work. The program now offers grants up to **$7,500** for resilient mitigation work, and the state notes that projects aligned with **IBHS FORTIFIED Roof** standards may also offer insurance-related benefits. For a Charleston roofer, that creates a real opportunity to publish useful content around storm hardening, fortified roofing, grant eligibility, and homeowner preparedness instead of just posting generic promotions. ([Department of Insurance South Carolina][4])

That is one of the biggest strategic advantages here: your content can be tied directly to things Charleston homeowners already care about — wind resistance, water intrusion, insurance questions, storm prep, roof replacement timing, and resilient upgrades. ([Department of Insurance South Carolina][4])
